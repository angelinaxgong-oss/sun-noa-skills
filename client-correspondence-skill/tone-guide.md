# Tone Guide — SUN-NOA Client Correspondence

The professional register in BOTH languages works the same way: **indirectness and
depersonalized phrasing signal professionalism**. Concretely:
- Use the **passive / indirect form for arrangements, instructions, and requests**
  (that is where it sounds sophisticated and neutral).
- Keep **active voice for warmth** ("thank you", "looking forward", "glad to").
- If everything is passive, a short message turns cold — or sounds like an auto-notice.
  Balance is the whole trick.

---

## 1. ENGLISH — FORMAL register (default for clients)

### Core patterns
| Purpose | Direct (avoid) | Formal (use) |
|---|---|---|
| Arrangements | "I'll speak English in the meeting." | "English will be used as the main language during the meeting." |
| Permission / invitation | "You can reply in Chinese." | "Chinese is always welcome whenever you prefer." / "Please feel free to reply in Chinese." |
| Requests | "Send me the documents." | "Could you kindly send…?" / "It would be appreciated if the documents could be sent…" |
| Thanks | "Thanks for your reply." | "Thank you for your reply — it is much appreciated." |
| Confirming | "Yes, ok." | "Our side has no objection, and it can be arranged as proposed." |
| Softening / hedging | "This is a problem." | "We would like to share some market feedback…" / "It seems that…" |
| Closing | "See you." | "It will be a pleasure to connect with you on…" / "Looking forward to…" |

### Structural habits
- Reply to a numbered client message **with the same numbering** — it shows care.
- One idea per sentence. Short sentences are more formal than long run-ons.
- Keep warmth active: end with a human, forward-looking line, not another passive.

### Opening / closing bank (formal)
- Openers: "Dear Mr. [Name],"; "Thank you for your message / for arranging the call."
- Closers: "Best regards," / "Kind regards," then name. Never "Sincerely" for WeChat.
- For first exchanges, "Dear Mr. Inagaki" is fine; "Hi Mr. Inagaki" is the lighter end of formal.

## 2. ENGLISH — CASUAL-FORMAL register (established contacts, logistics)

Same politeness, lower formality:
- Contractions allowed: "we'll", "it's", "thanks".
- Shorter sentences, friendlier verbs: "Just to confirm…", "Happy to help.", "Sounds good."
- Still NO: slang, "no worries 😊" everywhere, excessive exclamation marks in business.
- Still use passive-indirect for anything that is a real request or an arrangement the client
  depends on ("The files will be sent by…").

| Formal | Casual-formal |
|---|---|
| "It is much appreciated." | "Thanks — that's really helpful." |
| "English will be used as the main language." | "We'll keep the meeting in English." |
| "It will be a pleasure to connect with you." | "Looking forward to talking with you." |

**Deciding F vs C:** unknown/formal context → F. If the user says "we chat casually with this
contact", or "make it less formal", or the topic is routine (logistics, times, documents) → C.

## 3. CHINESE — authentic business formulas

The user's Chinese business voice is built by **mimicking established formulas**, not by
translating English. Rules:
1. Build every message from the formula library in `glossary.md` (by intent).
2. Keep sentences short and complete. CN business writing likes shorter clauses.
3. Address properly: WeChat/GC "稻垣先生，您好！" — name first, then 您好. Emails may add 尊敬的.
4. When confirming, mirror the client's structure and use 确认如下 / 按此执行.
5. Politeness markers to use naturally (not stacked): 请、烦请、如方便、随时欢迎、以…为准、
   届时、敬请、麻烦您.
6. AVOID: literal English-to-Chinese structures, Western punctuation habits (em-dashes),
   words like "第一点我们同意" without 我们/我方, and copying AI-style phrasing
   ("如果您有任何问题，请随时与我们联系" every single time).
7. Warm fillers are OK and human: 完全没问题、慢慢来、不着急、没问题的 — use sparingly.
8. Junior-to-boss-in-GC: attribute decisions to 我方/领导 (see Decision Gates in SKILL.md).

Example of CN built from formulas (numbered confirmation):
> 稻垣先生，您好！三点均已收到，确认如下：
> 1. 交流语言：按此前商定的方式执行即可，没有问题。
> 2. 交流平台：同意以微信作为主要联系方式。重要内容（如报价单、会议纪要）我方会整理成单独文件后通过微信发送，方便保存查阅。
> 3. 定期会议：非常赞同，可按每季度至少一次的频率安排，具体时间与议题每次提前沟通确定即可。
> 以上如无其他问题，期待周一的会议。谢谢！

## 4. ANTI-AI CHECKLIST

A message reads machine-made when it has several of these — remove them:
- [ ] Bullet points / bold labels inside a short WeChat message (fine only in long emails or structured confirmations that mirror the client's numbering)
- [ ] Over-parallel sentences (every sentence the same length & shape)
- [ ] Template openings and closings repeated verbatim every message
- [ ] Excessive hedging ("I think it might possibly be a good idea to perhaps…")
- [ ] Perfect grammar with zero contractions in a channel where humans write loosely
- [ ] Stacked politeness (请随时…如有任何问题…敬请… 全部堆在同一句)
- [ ] No personality: every message could have been sent to anyone
- [ ] Em-dash overuse; "；" chains in CN; semicolon symphonies in EN

**Human-sounding fixes:** vary sentence length; write the way the person would talk if calm
and professional; keep one casual beat per message (a "其实…", a "that's great to hear", a
"🙂" only when the user uses emojis); read it aloud — if you wouldn't say it, rewrite.

## 5. ANNOTATED EXAMPLES (what & why)

### Example A — warm reply to a client's personal note (casual-formal, WeChat)
EN: "Thank you for your message, and no worries at all — writing in Chinese with AI support
is still great practice. For listening and speaking, let's take it slowly. During the meeting
English will be used, and you are welcome to type in Chinese anytime. See you Monday."
Why: active voice for warmth at the start/end; passive-indirect only for the arrangement
("English will be used"); short sentences; no bullet points.

### Example B — formal numbered confirmation in a group chat (F)
CN: see §3 example. EN equivalent:
"Dear Mr. Inagaki, thank you for your message. All three points are confirmed as follows:
1) Language: as previously agreed — no issues. 2) Platform: WeChat is agreed as the main
channel; important documents will be compiled into single files and sent via WeChat for easy
saving and reference. 3) Regular meetings: fully agreed; they can be arranged at least once a
quarter, with details confirmed each time. Looking forward to the meeting on Monday."
Why: mirrors the client's numbering; arrangements passive ("will be compiled", "can be
arranged"); warmth active at the close.

### Example C — delivering uncomfortable market feedback (F, diplomatic)
EN: "We would also like to share some market feedback: for our previously cooperating
products (e.g., EPI), the cost is relatively high compared with equivalent domestic products,
which limits price competitiveness and further sales growth. We would be glad to hear your
views, and whether any adjustment is under consideration."
Why: feedback is framed as an observation ("would like to share"), the hard fact is stated
neutrally, and the ask is soft ("be glad to hear your views").

## 6. FORMAL GRAMMAR MOVES (corporate English toolbox)

Three moves used together create the "adult" register sentence:
**[inverted conditional] + [polite invitation] + [passive promise]**
> Should anything be missing or inaccurate, please feel free to let us know, and it will be
> revised promptly.

### 6.1 Inverted conditionals (drop "if", invert the verb)
| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Should + subject + verb | If … (possibility, tentative) | Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact us. |
| Were + subject + to + verb | If … were to (hypothetical) | Were we to proceed, the timeline would need to be agreed first. |
| Had + subject + past participle | If … had (past unreal) | Had we received the files earlier, the quotation could have been issued sooner. |

Negation stays after the subject: "Should you **not** require…", "Had it **not** been for…".
CN feel: 如有…请…；如蒙…；若非…。These read formal and slightly tentative — the reader
can decline gracefully. Use in emails and formal GC confirmations; never in casual WeChat
chatter.

### 6.2 Agentless passive for arrangements & actions
Pattern: **subject + will be / has been / is being + past participle** (agent omitted).
"will be sent", "has been received", "is being finalised", "it will be revised promptly".
Purpose: sounds official and neutral; avoids pointing at anyone. Use ACTIVE for warmth
("Thank you", "Looking forward").

### 6.3 Polite invitation & request openers
- "Please feel free to…" / "Please do not hesitate to…"
- "It would be appreciated if…" / "We would be grateful if…"
- "Could you kindly…?"
CN feel: 请随时…；如方便，烦请…；我们将不胜感激，如果您…

### 6.4 Softening (hedging) for sensitive topics
"It appears that…", "It seems that…", "would/could/might", "tend to", "I would suggest
that…". Purpose: disagreement and criticism become observations, not accusations.
CN feel: 据了解…；或许可以…；供您参考。

### 6.5 Light nominalisation (use sparingly)
Turning a verb into a noun adds weight: "will be given due consideration" (vs "will be
considered"), "arrangements have been made" (vs "we arranged"). WARNING: stack too many and
the sentence turns bureaucratic or AI-sounding — one per message max.

### 6.6 Formal connectors (email mainly)
Further to our call… / With reference to… / as requested / as agreed / at your earliest
convenience / in due course / Please find attached…（随函附上…，请查收）.
Avoid in short WeChat messages; reserve for emails and cover letters.

### 6.7 Anti-pattern warning
Never apply every move to one sentence. One inverted conditional OR one nominalisation per
message; keep one active-warmth beat. If every sentence is "Should you… it would be
appreciated if it were…", the message reads like a legal letter — or an AI pretending to
be one.
